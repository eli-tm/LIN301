Part 5 - The Verdict

The novels I chose are very different from each other. The Wizard of Oz is a classic fairytale story for children with fantasy creatures and magic. The King in Yellow is supernatural lovecraftian horror with drama and romance. The styles of both authors are different for each of their audiences and genres. The King in Yellow is longer, more complex, and judging from the top 10 content words, contains a lot of imagery and more “showing” instead of “telling.” The Wizard of Oz is shorter, less complex, and restates its character names more often, perhaps “telling” more than “showing.” The TTR and content words were the most revealing of the differences between the two novels. The TTR showed that, even when accounting for the different novel lengths, the King in Yellow has a wider vocabulary. The least telling was the affix comparison. This was because how many occurences of a particular affix didn’t reveal much about the authors’ styles, and it was also difficult to pick affixes that accurately represented themselves. For example, I chose the affix -able, but I’m wondering if the data would be different if I also measured for -ible, since they are the same. It might’ve been interesting to measure how many words/verbs were in present vs. past tense, but because of how many variation of tense affixes there are, I wouldn’t get much information from one or two affix comparisons. If I was given a random chapter from either novel, I would check the chapter’s top content words to find out which novel it’s from. The content words will be the most revealing on what the chapter is about, which will help me tell if it’s from the fairytale or horror story.

### Part 1 - Setup and Cleaning
I chose the novels *The King in Yellow* and *The Wizard of Oz* for my analysis. The following code is how I set up *The King in Yellow.*
```{python}
with open("../../data/gutenberg/king_yellow.txt", "r", encoding="utf-8") as f:  #imports The King in Yellow text file as f
    king_lines = f.readlines()      #creates the variable king_lines as a list of every line in king_yellow.txt

while not king_lines[0].startswith("                      THE REPAIRER OF REPUTATIONS"):       #the heading of the first chapter
    king_lines = king_lines[1:]     #gets rid of each line until it reaches the line that starts with the heading
king_lines = king_lines[1:]         #gets rid of the heading

print(king_lines[0:5])      #double checking the above code worked

while not king_lines[-1].startswith("*** END"):
    king_lines = king_lines[:-1]    #chop off the last line until it reaches *** END
king_lines = king_lines[:-1]        #chop off the *** END line itself

print(king_lines[-5:])      #double checking the above code worked

king_text = "".join(king_lines)     #joins all the lines into 1 text instead of a list

import re       #importing RegEx

king_words = re.split(r"[\W]+", king_text.lower())      #splitting the text word-by-word and making all characters lowercase
king_words = [w for w in king_words if w != ""]      #gets rid of all empty words

print(king_words[0:200])        #sampling the cleaned text!
```

## Part 2 - Tokens, Types, and Fair Comparison
The following code calculates the TTR of both complete novels and prints the results. 
``` {python}
king_tokens = len(king_words)       #calculates the total number of words
king_types = len(set(king_words))   #calculates the number of unique words
king_ttr = king_types/king_tokens   #calculates the total number of words divided by unique words to get the token-type ratio

oz_tokens = len(oz_words)           #repeating the above for the second novel
oz_types = len(set(oz_words))
oz_ttr = oz_types/oz_tokens

#printing the results:
print("The King in Yellow has", king_tokens, "words,", king_types, "unique words, and a", king_ttr, "TTR.")
print("The Wizard of Oz has", oz_tokens, "words,", oz_types, "unique words, and a", oz_ttr, "TTR.")
print("     The King in Yellow has a richer vocabulary.")

king_ttr_10000 = len(set(king_words[:10000])) / len(king_words[:10000])     #calculates the TTR of the first 10,000 words of king_words
oz_ttr_10000 = len(set(oz_words[:10000])) / len(oz_words[:10000])           #calculates the TTR of the first 10,000 words of oz_words

#printing the results:
print("The King in Yellow TTR (10,000 words):", king_ttr_10000)
print("The Wizard of Oz TTR (10,000 words):", oz_ttr_10000)
print("     The King in Yellow has a richer vocabulary within 10,000 words.")

king_wc = 0                         #creating a word count of 0
for w in king_words:                #for each word,
    king_wc += len(w)               #increase the word count by the length of the word
king_awl = king_wc/len(king_words)  #divide the word count by the total number of words to get the average word length

oz_wc = 0                           #repeat for the second novel
for w in oz_words:
    oz_wc += len(w)
oz_awl = oz_wc/len(oz_words)

#printing the results:
print("The King in Yellow has an average word length of", king_awl, "characters.")
print("The Wizard of Oz has an average word length of", oz_awl, "characters.")
```

### Common Words
The following code is how I analyzed the word lists of each novel to find the top 10 content words for each.
``` {python}
from collections import Counter

stopwords = ["the", "to", "and", "of", "a", "her", "i", "in", "was", "it",
             "she", "he", "be", "that", "you", "not", "had", "as", "his", "for",
             "with", "is", "have", "but", "at", "so", "all", "my", "been", "him",
             "on", "by", "could", "would", "very", "no", "what", "which", "they",
             "were", "there", "me", "an", "must", "this", "said", "from", "or",
             "will", "any", "much", "than", "such", "their", "them", "if", "do",
             "did", "one", "when", "your", "more", "are", "we", "who", "up",
             "out", "down", "into", "s", "t", "am", "then"]

king_counts = {}                #create empty dictionary
for w in king_words:            #for each word in king_words
    if w in king_counts:        #if the word is in the dictionary
        king_counts[w] += 1     #increase the value by 1
    else:                       #if the word is not in the dictionary
        king_counts[w] = 1      #do not increase the value 

king_counter = Counter(king_words)          #counts all the word appearances in king_words
king_top10 = king_counter.most_common(10)   #creates a variable of the 10 highest valued items
print(king_top10)

king_top_words = [pair[0] for pair in king_top10]       #pairs words and frequencies into a dictionary
king_top_freqs = [pair[1] for pair in king_top10] 

print(king_top_words)
print(king_top_freqs)

king_content_words = [w for w in king_words if w not in stopwords]      #adds words to variable if the words are not within the list stopwords

king_content_top10 = Counter(king_content_words).most_common(10)        #counts all the word appearances in king_content_words
king_content_labels = [pair[0] for pair in king_content_top10]          #pairs words and frequencies into a dictionary
king_content_freqs = [pair[1] for pair in king_content_top10]


oz_counts = {}                  #repeat for second novel
for w in oz_words:
    if w in oz_counts:
        oz_counts[w] += 1
    else:
        oz_counts[w] = 1

oz_counter = Counter(oz_words)
oz_top10 = oz_counter.most_common(10)
print(oz_top10)

oz_top_words = [pair[0] for pair in oz_top10] 
oz_top_freqs = [pair[1] for pair in oz_top10] 

print(oz_top_words)
print(oz_top_freqs)

oz_content_words = [w for w in oz_words if w not in stopwords]

oz_content_top10 = Counter(oz_content_words).most_common(10)
oz_content_labels = [pair[0] for pair in oz_content_top10]
oz_content_freqs = [pair[1] for pair in oz_content_top10]

print("The top 10 words in The King in Yellow are", king_content_labels, "with counts of", king_content_freqs)
print("The top 10 words in The Wizard of Oz are", oz_content_labels, "with counts of", oz_content_freqs)
```
#### Results:
The top 10 words in The King in Yellow are ['looked', 'over', 'clifford', 'know', 'eyes', 'about', 'now', 'came', 'again', 'like'] with counts of [162, 160, 156, 152, 145, 142, 141, 136, 135, 131]
    This list suggests the most important character name in the novel is Clifford, and perhaps eyes are a recurring image or theme.

The top 10 words in The Wizard of Oz are ['dorothy', 'scarecrow', 'woodman', 'lion', 'oz', 'great', 'little', 'tin', 'witch', 'asked'] with counts of [367, 224, 182, 178, 158, 140, 139, 139, 127, 114]
    This lists shows the names of the major characters such as Dorothy, Scarecrow, Lion, Oz, Tin Woodman (which appear as 2 items), and witch (but not wicked witch!). Dorothy's dog's name is not included.

## Part 5 - The Verdict
The novels I chose are very different from each other. The Wizard of Oz is a classic fairytale story for children with fantasy creatures and magic. The King in Yellow is supernatural lovecraftian horror with drama and romance. The styles of both authors are different for each of their audiences and genres. The King in Yellow is longer, more complex, and judging from the top 10 content words, contains a lot of imagery and more "showing" instead of "telling." The Wizard of Oz is shorter, less complex, and restates its character names more often, perhaps "telling" more than "showing."
The TTR and content words were the most revealing of the differences between the two novels. The TTR showed that, even when accounting for the different novel lengths, the King in Yellow has a wider vocabulary. The least telling was the affix comparison. This was because how many occurences of a particular affix didn't reveal much about the authors' styles, and it was also difficult to pick affixes that accurately represented themselves. For example, I chose the affix -able, but I'm wondering if the data would be different if I also measured for -ible, since they are the same. It might've been interesting to measure how many words/verbs were in present vs. past tense, but because of how many variation of tense affixes there are, I wouldn't get much information from one or two affix comparisons. 
If I was given a random chapter from either novel, I would check the chapter's top content words to find out which novel it's from. The content words will be the most revealing on what the chapter is about, which will help me tell if it's from the fairytale or horror story.
