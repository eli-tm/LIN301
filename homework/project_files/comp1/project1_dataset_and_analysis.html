What Counts as a Dataset?

You may choose one of the following (or propose another with my approval):

  • A short text or small collection of texts (literary works, news, speeches, transcripts, recipes, social media, etc.).
  • A word list (e.g., a Swadesh list, vocabulary from a textbook, your own collection).
  • A small corpus (or an excerpt of a larger corpus).
  • Data from an online resource (e.g., Concepticon, a dictionary, an archive).

The data can be in any language, or in more than one language. The one hard requirement is that it has to be available as plain text, or something easily convertible to plain text (a .txt, a .csv, a web page you can copy or download). A scanned PDF or a set of images is not easily convertible, so avoid it.

Keep it small and manageable: roughly 100–1,000 words is a good target. You can use a bigger set if your question needs one, but you can always scale up later, and scaling down in November is much harder.

WarningA note on copyright

Your LIN_301 repo is public. Public-domain texts (anything on Project Gutenberg, for instance) and openly licensed data are fine to upload. Copyrighted material, such as current song lyrics, recent novels, or paywalled news, can still be analyzed, but you shouldn’t post the full text in a public repo. If that’s your plan, mention it in your write-up and we’ll work out how to handle it.

Types of Analysis

You haven’t learned all of these tools yet, and that’s fine. Pick the direction(s) that fit your question, and we’ll fill in the how as the semester goes on.

Type of analysis What it asks Tools (where we learn them)
Frequency & vocabulary Which words are most common? How rich is the vocabulary (type-token ratio)? lists, sets, dictionaries (Python unit)
Pattern matching How often do certain spellings, sounds, affixes, or word shapes occur? regular expressions (command line unit), re in Python
Comparison How do two authors, texts, time periods, or languages differ? pandas; visualizations
Grammatical analysis What parts of speech, lemmas, or sentence structures show up? spaCy
Semantic analysis Which meanings or semantic fields are represented, and how are they related? WordNet
Cross-linguistic / non-English How do the patterns look in another language, or across translations? spaCy & WordNet for other languages

Most good projects combine two of these, for example frequency plus comparison, or pattern matching plus semantic analysis.

Example Topics

  • A set of tweets in Spanish (looking at slang or code-switching).
  • A Swadesh word list for Quechua (analyzing sound correspondences).
  • A short story in English (examining word frequency, sentence length, etc.).
  • A glossary from an online dictionary (tracking semantic fields).
  • Two translations of the same text (comparing vocabulary and word choice across translators).
  • A set of recipes (what verbs of cooking are used, and how often?).
